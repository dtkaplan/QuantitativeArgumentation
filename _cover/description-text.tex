{\large\bf Quantitative Argumentation} 

\medskip

In our contemporary age---so tied up with computing and data---quantitative measurement, 
description, and decision-making penetrate a huge variety of 
undertakings including business, government, social science, biology and medicine, 
and countless other areas. Quantitative reasoning skills are essential for organizing and extracting
information from data while taking into account what we already know about the world.

\medskip

Our English word "quantitative" stems from the Latin {\em quantus}, but the root is much 
older, dating to 4500-2500 BC and meaning "how much?" or "how many?" 
In short, "quantitative" starts as a question. 

\medskip

This book sets quantitative reasoning within the scientific method, even when applied to pressing
social, economic, and health-related decisions. Key to the scientific 
method is the notation of "hypotheses."
And key to quantitative reasoning is the ability to compare and compete different hypotheses
with one another, seeking those that provide us with the most valuable perspective on the situation
we are working with.

\medskip

Dealing with hypotheses quantitatively requires mathematical tools and perspectives that have 
been developed well after the development of the topics of high-school math education.
