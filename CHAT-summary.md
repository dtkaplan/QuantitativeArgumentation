# Generating a summary of a chapter

Each chapter will end with a short section that helps a student recall the chapter's purpose, central ideas, and practical significance.

- Write two to five bullets that can be read in about one minute.
- Begin with the chapter's central claim, framework, or question when that can be stated concisely.
- State key definitions in the book's own terminology.
- Preserve distinctions that students might otherwise confuse.
- Explain the consequence of an idea when relevant: what it permits, constrains, or helps the reader do.
- Do not retell examples or reproduce derivations. A brief reference to a named example is allowed only when it helps anchor a central idea.
- Do not introduce terminology, claims, or qualifications that are not developed in the chapter.
- Use direct, concrete language; each bullet should express one learnable idea.

Example for Chap 3:
* Quantities consist of a number and a unit.
* Dimension refers to the intrinsic character of the unit, e.g. "length" as opposed to the specific unit such as cm or miles.
* Addition, subtraction, and comparison of two quantities is only meaningful if they have the same dimension. However, multiplication and division are always valid.
